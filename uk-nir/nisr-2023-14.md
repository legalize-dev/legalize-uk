# The Organ and Tissue Donation (Deemed Consent) (2022 Act) (Commencement) Order (Northern Ireland) 2023

Made: 13th February 2023

The Department of Health[^f00001], in exercise of the powers conferred by section 2(2) of the Organ and Tissue Donation (Deemed Consent) Act (Northern Ireland) 2022[^f00002], makes the following Order.

#### Citation

###### 1

This Order may be cited as the Organ and Tissue Donation (Deemed Consent) (2022 Act) (Commencement) Order (Northern Ireland) 2023.

#### Provisions coming into operation on 13th February 2023

###### 2

The 13th February 2023 is the appointed day for the coming into operation of section 1 (“Appropriate Consent” to adult transplant activities). of the Organ and Tissue Donation (Deemed Consent) Act (Northern Ireland) 2022.

## Signed

Sealed with the Official Seal of the Department of Health on 13th February 2023

Peter May — A senior officer of the Department of Health

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Order)

EXPLANATORY NOTE

This Commencement Order is made by the Department of Health under section 2(2) of the Organ and Tissue Donation (Deemed Consent) Act (Northern Ireland) 2022 (“*the Act*”).

It brings into operation section 1 of the Act (“appropriate consent” to adult transplant activities) on 05 April 2023. This section amends section 3 of the Human Tissue Act [2004 (c. 30)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/30) to provide that deemed consent of the person concerned amounts to appropriate consent for the purposes of certain transplantation activities unless the person concerned is an excepted adult. It shifts the default position in relation to consent away from an opt-in system to an opt-out one.

The section also amends section 15 of the Health (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act (Northern Ireland) 2016 to include a duty on the Department to inform the public at least once a year specifically about deemed consent and how a person can during their lifetime record their own decisions about transplantation after their death or parts of their body, or appoint someone to make decisions after their death about transplantation of parts of their body.
