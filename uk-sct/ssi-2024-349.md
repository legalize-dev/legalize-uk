# The Free-Range Egg Marketing Standards (Amendment) (Scotland) Regulations 2024

Made: 19th November 2024

Coming into force: 20th November 2024

In accordance with section 9(4) of the Agriculture (Retained EU Law and Data) (Scotland) Act 2020 a draft of this instrument has been laid before and approved by resolution of the Scottish Parliament.

#### Citation and commencement

###### 1

These Regulations may be cited as the Free-Range Egg Marketing Standards (Amendment) (Scotland) Regulations 2024 and come into force on the day after the day on which they are made.

#### Amendment of Commission Regulation (EC) No 589/2008

###### 2

- (1) [Commission Regulation (EC) No 589/2008](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2008/0589) laying down detailed rules for implementing Council Regulation [(EC) No 1234/2007](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2007/1234) as regards marketing standards for eggs[^f00003] is amended as follows.

- (2) In Annex 2 (minimum requirements for systems of production for the various egg farming methods), in paragraph 1, in the second sub-paragraph, in point (a)—

- (a) for “retained direct EU legislation” substitute “assimilated law”,

- (b) before “restricted” in the first place it occurs insert “temporarily”,

- (c) omit “, provided” to “restricted” in the third place it occurs.

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

EXPLANATORY NOTE

These Regulations amend [Commission Regulation (EC) No. 589/2008](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/european/regulation/2008/0589) (“EUR 2008/589”) which lays down detailed rules as regards marketing standards for eggs.

Regulation 2 amends EUR 2008/589, paragraph 1, second sub-paragraph in point (a) of Annex 2 (minimum requirements for systems of production for the various egg farming methods). The amendment removes the 16 week limit on the length of time packs of Class A eggs can continue to be marked as “free-range” where the laying hens’ daytime access to open-air runs is restricted. It provides that eggs can be marked as “free-range” notwithstanding any temporary restriction imposed on the basis of assimilated law to protect public or animal health.

A Business and Regulatory Impact Assessment and a Child Rights and Wellbeing Impact Assessment have been prepared and are available online at www.legislation.gov.uk.
