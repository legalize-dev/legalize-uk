# The Mental Health (Care and Treatment) (Scotland) Act 2003 Remedial Order 2026

Made: 11th November 2026

Coming into force: 13th November 2026

The Scottish Ministers make the following Order in exercise of the powers conferred by section 12(1) and (3) of the Convention Rights (Compliance) (Scotland) Act 2001[^f00001](“*the 2001 Act*”) and all other powers enabling them to do so.

The Scottish Ministers consider the provision made by this remedial Order to be necessary or expedient in consequence of the provisions in the Mental Health (Care and Treatment) (Scotland) Act 2003[^f00002] in relation to the inability to specify recorded matters in compulsion orders, compulsion and restriction orders, hospital directions and transfer for treatment directions which is or may be incompatible with Convention rights[^f00003].

In accordance with section 12(2) of the 2001 Act the Scottish Ministers are of the opinion that there are compelling reasons for making a remedial order as distinct from taking any other action.

In accordance with section 13(3) of the 2001 Act the Scottish Ministers laid before the Scottish Parliament a copy of the proposed draft Order, together with a statement of their reasons for proposing to make the Order, gave such public notice of the proposed draft Order as they considered appropriate, invited observations on it and had regard to observations submitted.

In accordance with section 13(4) of the 2001 Act the Scottish Ministers laid before the Scottish Parliament a statement summarising all the observations to which they had regard under section 13(3)(c) and specifying the changes which they made in the draft Order and the reasons for them.

In accordance with section 13(2) of that Act, a draft of this instrument has been laid before and approved by resolution of the Scottish Parliament.

## Part 1 — Introductory

#### Citation, commencement and interpretation

###### 1

- (1) This Order may be cited as the Mental Health (Care and Treatment) (Scotland) Act 2003 Remedial Order 2026.

- (2) This Order comes into force on 13 November 2026.

- (3) In this Order, “*the 2003 Act*” means the Mental Health (Care and Treatment) (Scotland) Act 2003.

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Order)

Explanatory Note

This Order principally amends the Mental Health Care and Treatment (Scotland) Act 2003 (“*the 2003 Act*”) to remove an incompatibility with the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR). The ability of the Mental Health Tribunal for Scotland (“*the Tribunal*”) to specify and subsequently modify, “recorded matters” in a “civil” order such as a community treatment order but not in a “forensic” order such as a compulsion order was found to violate article 14 (protection from discrimination) of the ECHR in the case of X v Mental Health Tribunal for Scotland [2022] CSOH 78.

In order to address the incompatibility this Order amends Parts 9 (compulsion orders), 10 (compulsion orders and restriction orders) and 11 (hospital directions and transfer for treatment directions) of the 2003 Act. The Order enables recorded matters to be varied (added, amended or removed) during reviews of compulsion orders, compulsion orders with restriction orders, hospital directions and transfer for treatment directions.

The Order creates duties on responsible medical officers and on the Scottish Ministers to take steps, consult key persons and, where appropriate, make applications or references to the Tribunal when modification of recorded matters may be required or where recorded matters are not being provided, and confers corresponding powers on the Tribunal to vary recorded matters when determining relevant applications, reviews or references.

**Amendments to Part 9 of the 2003 Act (compulsion orders)**

Article 2 of this Order amends sections 145, 150, 154, 155, 156, 158, 159, 164, 166, 167, 168, 169, 171, 172, 175 and inserts sections 161A and 161B.

Section 145 (mandatory reviews: further steps to be taken where compulsion order not revoked) is amended to add a requirement for the responsible medical officer to consider whether recorded matters in a compulsion order should be modified during a further review of the order.

Section 154 (responsible medical officer’s duty where extension and variation proposed) is amended to introduce a requirement that the responsible medical officer, when conducting a further review of the compulsion order, proposes changing the order by modifying recorded matters. Subsection (3) provides that the Mental Health Officer must be notified of a proposed application to the Tribunal and of the proposed changes to recorded matters.

Section 155 (mental health officer’s duties: extension and variation of compulsion order) sets out the duties of the mental health officer following notification under section 154(3) and section 156 (responsible medical officer’s duty to apply for extension or variation of compulsion order) places a duty on the responsible medical officer to apply to the Tribunal where they are satisfied the compulsion order should remain in place and that the order be varied. Both sections are amended to add in references to recorded matters, reflecting the fact that the order can be varied by modifying a recorded matter in addition to a measure. Likewise, section 158 (application to tribunal for extension and variation of compulsion order) is amended to ensure that such variation can include modification of a recorded matter, in addition to a measure.

Section 159 (responsible medical officer’s duties: variation of compulsion order) requires the responsible medical officer to consider on an ongoing basis whether the measures specified in a compulsion order need to be varied. Amendments are therefore made so that references to considering measures will include consideration of recorded matters.

New section 161A (recorded matters: reference to Tribunal by responsible medical officer) requires the responsible medical officer to make a reference to the Tribunal if any recorded matter is not being delivered to the patient. The responsible medical officer must seek the views of the mental health officer and such other individuals the responsible medical officer considers appropriate. The responsible medical officer must make a reference to the Tribunal unless they are making an application to revoke the compulsion order, or they have made an application to extend or vary the order.

New section 161B (reference to Tribunal under section 161A(3): notification) imposes notification requirements on the responsible medical officer where they have made a reference under section 161A. Persons to be notified are: the patient, their named person, any guardian and/or welfare attorney, the mental health officer and the Mental Welfare Commission.

Section 164 (applications to Tribunal by patient etc. for revocation or variation of compulsion order) allows patients and their named persons to apply to the Tribunal to vary a compulsion order by modifying measures. Section 166 (powers of the Tribunal on review under section 165) concerns the Tribunal’s powers in relation to a responsible medical officer’s determination to extend a compulsion order, including the modification of any measures. Both sections are amended to add in modification of recorded matters in addition to measures.

Section 167 (powers of the Tribunal on application under section 149, 158, 161, 163 or 164) is amended to give the Tribunal power when extending or varying the order to specify a recorded matter and modify recorded matters. Section 168 (interim extension etc. of order: application under section 149 or 158) allows the Tribunal to make interim orders that extend and vary compulsion orders by modifying measures for up to 28 days. Amendments are made to ensure the Tribunal can make interim orders varying a compulsion order by modifying recorded matters.

Section 169 (interim variation of order following application, reference or review under Chapter) allows the Tribunal to make interim variations to compulsion orders by modifying measures while an application, reference or review is pending. An interim order varying the compulsion order by modifying measures can be made for a period not exceeding 28 days. Amendments ensure that interim orders can include modification of recorded matters in addition to measures. The section is also amended to ensure that it applies to a reference under section 161A regarding non-provision of a recorded matter.

Section 171 (powers of the Tribunal under section 161A or 162) is amended to allow the Tribunal to vary or revoke a compulsion order upon a section 161A reference by the responsible medical officer which in addition to modifying measures includes modifying recorded matters.

Section 172 (tribunal’s order varying compulsion order) is amended to ensure that the Tribunal, after making a determination to vary a compulsion order, can specify any modifications to recorded matters as well as to include other recorded matters not in the original application.

Section 175 (meaning of modify) contains the definition of 'modify'. This is amended to include adding, removing, or amending recorded matters in a compulsion order. A further insertion also clarifies that the meaning of modify includes specifying a recorded matter in an order which does not specify a recorded matter.

Article 3 of this Order amends the Mental Health (Compulsion orders - documents and reports to be submitted to the Tribunal) (Scotland) Regulations 2005 (“*the 2005 Regulations*”). The 2005 Regulations provide that particular documents must accompany certain types of application to the Tribunal and set out reports that the Tribunal may require to be prepared and submitted to it in connection with certain applications or a particular determination. Article 3 inserts new regulation 4A (reference concerning non-provision of a recoded matter: report to be prepared and submitted to Tribunal). The effect is that the Tribunal can request a report and relevant information from the mental health officer on their views about the non-provision of a recorded matter which would assist the Tribunal in considering the application.

**Amendments to Part 10 of the 2003 Act (compulsion orders and restriction orders)**

Article 4 of this Order amends Part 10 of the 2003 Act to make parallel provision for cases where a compulsion order is accompanied by a restriction order.

Sections 182 (review of compulsion order and restriction order) and 184 (responsible medical officer’s duty to keep compulsion order and restriction order under review) are amended so that where certain conditions are met there is a duty placed upon the responsible medical officer to comply with section 184A. New section 184A (responsible medical officer’s duties: variation of compulsion order) is inserted to require the responsible medical officer, where the statutory criteria for continuing the order are met, to consider modification of any recorded matter, to assess the patient’s treatment needs, consult the mental health officer and others, and (if appropriate) to recommend variation of recorded matters in reports prepared for annual or other reviews.

New section 184B (recorded matters: report to Scottish Ministers by responsible medical officer) requires the responsible medical officer, where satisfied that a recorded matter is not being provided, to submit a report to the Scottish Ministers. New section 185A (duties of Scottish Ministers on receiving report from responsible medical officer under section 184B(3)) requires the Scottish Ministers to refer the case to the Tribunal upon receipt of such a report and to notify specified persons.

Section 188 (duty of Scottish Ministers to keep compulsion order and restriction order under review) is amended so that where certain conditions are met there is a duty placed upon the Scottish Ministers to comply with section 188A. Section 188A (the Scottish Ministers’ duties: variation of compulsion order) is inserted to require the Scottish Ministers, where the statutory criteria for continuing the order are met, to consider modification of recorded matters, undertake assessment and consultation, notify the responsible medical officer and mental health officer if they propose to apply to the Tribunal to vary recorded matters, and (if satisfied) to apply to the Tribunal. New sections 188B (recorded matters: Scottish Ministers’ duties), 188C (reference to Tribunal under section 188B(3): notification) and 188D (reference concerning non-provision of recorded matters: mental health officer report for Tribunal) create duties on the Scottish Ministers to consult and notify specified persons and to make a reference to the Tribunal where they are satisfied that a recorded matter is not being provided, and allow the Tribunal to require a mental health officer report when considering such references.

Section 190 (application by Scottish Ministers: notification) is amended to provide for notification duties in relation to applications made by the Scottish Ministers by virtue of new section 188A.

Sections 191 (application to Tribunal) and 192 (application to Tribunal by patient and named person) are amended so that patients and named persons may apply to vary recorded matters in a compulsion order with a restriction order, subject to frequency limits for such variation applications.

Section 193 (Powers of Tribunal on reference under section 185(1), 187(2) or 189(2) or application under section 191 or 192(2)) is amended to include references arising from the new duties. Subsection (7A) is inserted to require the Tribunal, where satisfied that the compulsion order and the restriction order should continue and that a recorded matter should be modified, to make an order varying the compulsion order accordingly.

Section 194 (Tribunal’s powers etc. when varying compulsion order) is amended consequentially to ensure that the Tribunal’s powers apply to an order varied under section 193(7A) concerning the modification of a recorded matter.

Section 199 (meaning of modify) expands the meaning of “modify” to include recorded matters. A further insertion also clarifies that the meaning of modify includes specifying a recorded matter in an order which does not specify a recorded matter.

**Amendments to Part 11 of the 2003 Act (hospital directions and transfer for treatment directions)**

Article 5 of this Order makes equivalent provision in Part 11 of the 2003 Act for hospital directions and transfer for treatment directions.

Sections 206 (review of hospital direction and transfer for treatment direction) and 208 (responsible medical officer’s duty to keep directions under review) are amended so that where certain conditions are met there is a duty placed upon the responsible medical officer to comply with section 208A. New section 208A (responsible medical officer’s duties: variation of direction) is inserted to require the responsible medical officer, where the statutory criteria for continuation are met, to consider modification of recorded matters, assess, consult, notify the mental health officer of proposed recommendations and include recommendations in reports to the Scottish Ministers.

New section 208B (recorded matters: report to Scottish Ministers by responsible medical officer) requires the responsible medical officer, where satisfied that a recorded matter is not being provided, to submit a report to the Scottish Ministers (subsection (3)). New Section 210A (duties of Scottish Ministers on receiving report from responsible medical officer under section 208B(3)) then requires the Scottish Ministers to refer the case to the Tribunal and to notify specified persons.

Section 212 (duty of Scottish Ministers to keep directions under review) is amended so that where certain conditions are met there is a duty placed upon the Scottish Ministers to comply with section 212A. New section 212A (the Scottish Ministers’ duties: variation of direction) is inserted to require the Scottish Ministers, where the statutory criteria for continuation are met, to consider modification of recorded matters, consult, notify and, if satisfied, apply to the Tribunal under new section 213B (application to Tribunal) for an order under section 215 to vary the direction by modifying any recorded matter specified in it.

New sections 212B (recorded matters: Scottish Ministers’ duties), 212C (reference to Tribunal under section 212B(3): notification) and 212D (reference concerning non-provision of recorded matters: mental health officer report for Tribunal) create consultation and reference duties for the Scottish Ministers where they are satisfied that a recorded matter is not being provided, and allow the Tribunal to require a mental health officer report.

New section 213A (application by Scottish Ministers: notification) provides for notifications where the Scottish Ministers by virtue of section 212A, makes an application under section 213B, and new section 213B (application to Tribunal) sets out the content of such an application.

Section 214 (application to Tribunal by patient and named person) is amended to allow patients and named persons to apply to vary a direction by modifying any recorded matter specified in it, subject to initial and annual frequency limits.

Section 215 (powers of Tribunal on reference under section 210(3), 211(2) or 213(2) or on application under section 214(2)) is amended to add the new routes of reference and application and to require the Tribunal, where satisfied that a direction should continue and that a recorded matter should be modified, to make an order varying the direction accordingly.

New section 217A (meaning of modify) clarifies the meaning of “modify” in this Chapter to include adding, amending, removing or newly specifying recorded matters.

**Meaning of “recorded matter”**

Article 6(2) of this Order amends section 64 of the 2003 Act (powers of Tribunal on application under section 63: compulsory treatment order) to make clear that, when making a compulsory treatment order, the Tribunal may specify a recorded matter.

Article 6(3) of this Order substitutes a new definition of “recorded matter” in section 329 of the 2003 Act (interpretation) so that it includes such medical treatment, community care services, relevant services, and any other treatment, care or service as the Tribunal considers appropriate.
