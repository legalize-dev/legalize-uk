# The Bus Services (Contract Terms) (Wales) Regulations 2026

Made: 26 November 2026

Coming into force: 23 January 2027

The Welsh Ministers make the following Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by [sections 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/11)[(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/11), [(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/11) and [42](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/42)[(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/42) of the [Bus Services (Wales) Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/contents)[^f00001].

## Part 1 — General

#### Title and coming into force

###### 1

- (1) The title of these Regulations is the Bus Services (Contract Terms) (Wales) Regulations 2026.

- (2) These Regulations come into force on 23 January 2027.

#### Interpretation

###### 2

- (1) In these Regulations—

- “*the Act*” (“*y Ddeddf*”) means the [Bus Services (Wales) Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/contents);

- “*contract*” (“*contract*”) means a local bus service contract entered into under [section 10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/10) of [the Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/contents);

- “*service*” (“*gwasanaeth*”) means a local bus service as defined in [section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/1) of [the Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/contents).

- (2) Words and expressions used in these Regulations and in [the Act](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/contents) have the same meaning as in that Act.

## Part 2 — Contracts: required terms

#### Service Particulars

###### 3

A contract must specify—

- (a) the service number of the service,

- (b) the days of operation of the service and any planned variations,

- (c) the time by which the service is required to start and finish on each day of operation and any planned variations,

- (d) whether or not the service is a cross-border service,

- (e) in the case of a standard local bus service—

- (i) the first embarkation point,

- (ii) the last disembarkation point,

- (iii) all other embarkation and disembarkation points including any sections of the route where passengers may request to embark onto or disembark from the service at any point along the route,

- (iv) the timetable of the service and any planned variations, and

- (v) the route of the service and any planned variations, and

- (f) in the case of a flexible local bus service—

- (i) the geographical area in which the service is required to operate,

- (ii) any fixed sections of route and any planned variations,

- (iii) any fixed embarkation and disembarkation points, and

- (iv) the location where vehicles used to provide the service will normally be kept when not in use.

#### Vehicles

###### 4

- (1) A contract must require an operator to ensure vehicles used to provide the service comply, so far as reasonably practicable, with the minimum passenger capacity specified in the contract.

- (2) For the purposes of [paragraph (1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/4/1/made), a contract may specify more than one minimum passenger capacity for the same service.

- (3) In this regulation, “*minimum passenger capacity*” means the minimum number of people, excluding the driver, that a vehicle must be able to carry.

#### Education and training

###### 5

A contract must require an operator to ensure—

- (a) staff engaged in the operation or provision of the service—

- (i) are trained to perform their role, and

- (ii) receive safeguarding training appropriate to their role which may include, among other things, training on crime prevention, anti-social behaviour, de-escalation techniques and procedures for incident management, and

- (b) bus drivers receive training on defensive driving, customer service and emergency procedures.

#### Safety

###### 6

- (1) A contract must require an operator—

- (a) to ensure that vehicles used to provide the service, so far as reasonably practicable, have a working closed circuit television system,

- (b) to record and report, without delay, any serious incident affecting the safety, health or welfare of one or more of the following groups—

- (i) passengers;

- (ii) staff engaged in the operation or provision of the service;

- (iii) members of the public,

- (c) to record and report, without delay, any incident of anti-social behaviour occurring on vehicles used to provide the service or in the vicinity of premises used for the purpose of providing the service, and

- (d) to have a safety management system in place.

- (2) A contract must contain a term about the arrangements for recording and reporting incidents.

- (3) A contract for a school service must prohibit an operator from permitting a person to drive a vehicle for a school service, except where condition 1 or 2 is met.

- (4) Condition 1 is that—

- (a) the operator has, within the past three years, checked a relevant enhanced criminal record certificate issued within that period relating to the person who is to drive the vehicle, and

- (b) the certificate does not show that the person is barred from regulated activity relating to children.

- (5) Condition 2 is that—

- (a) the operator has, within the past three years, checked—

- (i) a relevant enhanced criminal record certificate relating to the person who is to drive the vehicle, and

- (ii) up-date information given, within the past three years, under [section 116A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/50/section/116A) of the [Police Act 1997](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/50/contents)[^f00002] in relation to the certificate,

- (b) the certificate does not show that the person is barred from regulated activity relating to children, and

- (c) the up-date information is not advice to request the person to apply for a new enhanced criminal record certificate.

- (6) For the purposes of [paragraph (1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/6/1/made)[(d)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/6/1/d/made), “*safety management system*” means a system which includes standard operating procedures for vehicle maintenance, driver training, passenger safety and the handling of emergencies.

- (7) For the purposes of [paragraph (3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/6/3/made), “*school service*” means a service where—

- (a) the driver is engaging in regulated activity relating to children by virtue of [paragraph 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/schedule/4/paragraph/2)[(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/schedule/4/paragraph/2)[(f)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/schedule/4/paragraph/2) of [Schedule 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/schedule/4) to the [Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/contents)[^f00003],

- (b) the children using the service are being conveyed between their homes and institutions for providing primary education, secondary education or further education that they are attending, and

- (c) the vehicle used to provide the service requires a PSV operator’s licence for its use.

- (8) In [this regulation](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/6/made)—

- “*children*” (“*plant*”) means persons who have not attained the age of 18;

- “*enhanced criminal record certificate*” (“*tystysgrif cofnod troseddol fanwl*”) means an enhanced criminal record certificate issued under [section 113B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/50/section/113B) of the [Police Act 1997](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/50/contents)[^f00004];

- “*further education*” (“*addysg bellach*”), “*primary education*” (“*addysg gynradd*”) and “*secondary education*” (“*addysg uwchradd*”) have the same meanings as in [section 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/56/section/2) of the [Education Act 1996](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1996/56/contents)[^f00005];

- “*regulated activity relating to children*” (“*gweithgaredd a reoleiddir sy’n ymwneud â phlant*”) is to be construed in accordance with [Part 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/schedule/4/part/1) of [Schedule 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/schedule/4) to the [Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/47/contents)[^f00006];

- “*relevant enhanced criminal record certificate*” (“*tystysgrif cofnod troseddol fanwl berthnasol*”) means an enhanced criminal record certificate which includes, by virtue of [section 113BA](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/50/section/113BA) of the [Police Act 1997](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1997/50/contents)[^f00007], suitability information relating to children.

#### Record keeping

###### 7

A contract must require an operator to maintain records sufficient to demonstrate compliance with—

- (a) health and safety requirements, and

- (b) safeguarding requirements.

#### Payments

###### 8

A contract must specify—

- (a) when and how an operator will receive payment for providing the service, and

- (b) the circumstances in which additions to, or deductions from, payments made to an operator may be made, and how those additions or deductions will be applied.

#### Incentives for improving the quality of the service

###### 9

- (1) A contract must contain a term about the application of incentives for achieving the service levels specified in the contract.

- (2) A contract must specify at least one key performance indicator.

- (3) But [paragraph (2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/9/2/made) does not apply to a contract to which [section 52](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/52) of the [Procurement Act 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/contents) applies[^f00008].

- (4) For the purposes of [paragraph (2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/9/2/made), “*key performance indicator*” has the same meaning as in [section 52](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/52)[(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/section/52) of the [Procurement Act 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/54/contents).

#### Ticketing and fares

###### 10

- (1) A contract must contain terms about—

- (a) the supply and maintenance of ticketing machines and ticketing software,

- (b) the inspection of tickets, ticketing machines and the recording of information in relation to inspections, and

- (c) the acceptance of passenger fares, payments and tickets.

- (2) A contract must allocate responsibility for losses of revenue made in connection with the sale of tickets.

- (3) A contract for an eligible service must contain a term about the arrangements relating to the acceptance of statutory travel concession permits.

- (4) For the purposes of [paragraph (3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/wsi/2026/145/regulation/10/3/made)—

- “*eligible service*” (“*gwasanaeth cymwys*”) has the same meaning as in [article 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2002/1016/article/3) of the Travel Concessions (Eligible Services) Order 2002[^f00009];

- “*statutory travel concession permit*” (“*trwydded consesiwn teithio statudol*”) means a permit issued under [section 145B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/38/section/145B) of the [Transport Act 2000](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/38/contents)[^f00010].

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

Explanatory Note

These Regulations set out the terms which must be included in a local bus service contract entered into under [section 10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/section/10) of the [Bus Services (Wales) Act 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asc/2026/2/contents).

The required terms include terms relating to—

1. service particulars;

2. vehicles;

3. education and training;

4. safety;

5. record keeping;

6. payments;

7. incentives for improving the quality of the service;

8. ticketing and fares.

The Welsh Ministers’ Code of Practice on the carrying out of Regulatory Impact Assessments was considered in relation to these Regulations. As a result, a regulatory impact assessment has been prepared as to the likely costs and benefits of complying with these Regulations. A copy can be obtained from Welsh Government, Cathays Park, Cardiff, CF10 3NQ and is published on www.gov.wales.
