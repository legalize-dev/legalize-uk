# The Online Safety Act 2023 (Commencement No. 8) Regulations 2026

Made: 11th November 2026

The Secretary of State makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by section 240(1) and (2) of the Online Safety Act 2023[^f00001].

#### Citation and extent

###### 1

- (1) These Regulations may be cited as the Online Safety Act 2023 (Commencement No. 8) Regulations 2026.

- (2) These Regulations extend to England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

#### Provisions coming into force on 11th November 2026

###### 2

The following provisions of the Online Safety Act 2023 come into force on 11th November 2026—

- (a) section 75 (disclosure of information about use of service by deceased child users);

- (b) in section 23(11) (record-keeping and review duties: user-to-user services), in the definition of “relevant duties”, the reference to section 75 (deceased child users);

- (c) section 34 (record-keeping and review duties: search services), so far as not already commenced;

- (d) section 100(6)(a)(v) (power to require information);

- (e) section 104(13)(a)(xiv) (reports by skilled persons);

- (f) paragraphs 14 and 30 of Schedule 8 (transparency reports: matters about which information may be required).

## Signed

Ian Murray — Minister of State — Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport — 11th November 2026

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

Explanatory Note

These Regulations are the eighth commencement regulations made under the Online Safety Act [2023 (c. 50)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/50) (“*the Act*”). They bring into force section 75 of the Act which places duties on providers of Category 1, 2A and 2B services to set out their policies on disclosing information to the parents of deceased child users, have a dedicated helpline or section of the service (or similar means) for such parents, provide details about the relevant policies and procedures in their terms of service or a publicly available statement, and respond in a timely manner to requests for information. Section 75 also requires the providers to operate a complaints procedure in relation to these duties.

These Regulations also bring into force other provisions in the Act which correspond to the section 75 duties, such as duties on categorised services in relation to record keeping and the power of Ofcom to require information to assess compliance with the section 75 duties and include information about this in its annual transparency reports.
