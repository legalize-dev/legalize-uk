# The Inheritance Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1989

Made: 27th June 1989

Laid before the House of Commons: 29th June 1989

Coming into force: 1st August 1989

The Commissioners of Inland Revenue, in exercise of the powers conferred on them by section 256(1) of the Inheritance Tax Act 1984[^f00001], hereby make the following Regulations:

#### Citation, commencement and extent

###### 1

These Regulations may be cited as the Inheritance Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1989 and shall come into force on 1st August 1989.

###### 2

These Regulations extend to Northern Ireland only.

#### Interpretation

###### 3

In these Regulations “the Principal Regulations” means the Capital Transfer Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1981[^f00002].

#### Amendments to Principal Regulations

###### 4

In Regulation 3 of the Principal Regulations—

- (a) in paragraph (b) for “£70,000” there shall be substituted “£100,000”;

- (b) in paragraph (c) for “£10,000” there shall be substituted “£15,000”;

- (c) in paragraph (d) for “1st April 1987” there shall be substituted “1st April 1989”.

## Signed

A. J. G. Isaac — L. J. H. Beighton — Two of the Commissioners of Inland Revenue — 27th June 1989

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

The Principal Regulations (S.I. [1981/1441](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1981/1441)) as amended by the Inheritance Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1987 (S.I. [1987/1129](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1987/1129)) dispensed with the need to deliver an account for the purposes of inheritance tax where (subject to specified exceptions) the value of a deceased’s estate did not exceed £70,000 and where the deceased died on or after 1st April 1987. These Regulations increase that limit to £100,000 in respect of deaths on or after 1st April 1989. These Regulations also provide that up to £15,000 of value in respect of property situated outside the United Kingdom may be included in the £100,000 limit without losing “excepted estate” status.
