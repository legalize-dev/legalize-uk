# The Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (References to Financial Investigators) (England and Wales and Northern Ireland) (Amendment) Order 2026

Made: 24th November 2026

Laid before Parliament: 26th November 2026

Coming into force: 31st December 2026

The Secretary of State makes this Order in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 453(1) and (2) and 459(2) of the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002[^f00001].

#### Citation, commencement and extent

###### 1

- (1) This Order may be cited as the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (References to Financial Investigators) (England and Wales and Northern Ireland) (Amendment) Order 2026.

- (2) This Order comes into force on 31st December 2026.

- (3) This Order extends to England and Wales and Northern Ireland, apart from—

- (a) article 3, which extends to England and Wales, and

- (b) article 4, which extends to Northern Ireland.

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Order)

Explanatory Note

This Order amends Schedule 1 to the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (References to Financial Investigators) (England and Wales and Northern Ireland) Order 2021 ([S.I. 2021/640](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2021/640)) (“*the 2021 Order*”). The Proceeds of Crime Act [2002 (c. 29)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2002/29) (“*the 2002 Act*”) provides that accredited financial investigators may, in specified circumstances, discharge powers or other functions which are granted to law enforcement officers (such as, for example, police constables or Serious Fraud Office officers).

Section 453 of the 2002 Act allows the Secretary of State to provide that references in specified provisions of the 2002 Act to accredited financial investigators are to be read as references to accredited financial investigators of particular descriptions set out by order. Those descriptions are set out in the 2021 Order.

This Order amends the 2021 Order to include staff working for the Forestry Commission, the Immigration Advice Authority and the Intellectual Property Office among the descriptions of accredited financial investigators in specific cases.

Accredited financial investigators in the Forestry Commission will be able to exercise powers in Part 2 of the 2002 Act including making applications for restraint orders, search and seizure powers and authorisations for applications and appeals; Part 7 of the 2002 Act enabling senior officers to make applications to the court to extend a moratorium period; and Part 8 of the 2002 Act in relation to investigations, including applications for search and seizure warrants and confiscation investigations and money laundering investigations. The Forestry Commission’s remit extends to England only and the powers will only be exercisable in England.

Accredited financial investigators in the Immigration Advice Authority will be able to exercise powers in Part 2 of the 2002 Act including making applications for restraint orders, search and seizure powers and authorisations for applications and appeals. They will also be able to exercise equivalent powers in Northern Ireland under Part 4 of the 2002 Act. They will be able to exercise civil recovery powers in Part 5 of the 2002 Act including the recovery of cash in summary proceedings; prior approval by senior officer for search of cash; forfeiture notices; recovery of listed assets; freezing and forfeiture of money in accounts; cryptoassets and converted cryptoassets: searches, seizure, detention, freezing, and conversion; and forfeiture of cryptoassets. They will be able to exercise powers in Part 7 of the 2002 Act enabling senior officers to apply to the court to extend a moratorium period. They will be able to exercise powers in Part 8 of the 2002 Act in relation to investigations including making applications for search and seizure warrants; confiscation investigations; detained cash investigations; detained property investigations; frozen funds investigations; and money laundering investigations. Accredited financial investigators in the Immigration Advice Authority will be able to exercise these powers in relation to England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Accredited financial investigators in the Intellectual Property Office will be able to exercise powers in Part 5 of the 2002 Act including the recovery of cash in summary proceedings; prior approval by senior officer for search of cash; forfeiture notices; recovery of listed assets; freezing and forfeiture of money in accounts; cryptoassets and converted cryptoassets: searches, seizure, detention, freezing, and conversion; and forfeiture of cryptoassets. They will be able to exercise powers in Part 8 of the 2002 Act in relation to money laundering investigations. Accredited financial investigators in the Intellectual Property Office will be able to exercise these powers in relation to England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as no, or no significant, impact on the private, voluntary or public sectors is foreseen. An Economic Note has been prepared to assess the expected costs and benefits of the measure and provide proportionate analysis of its impacts.
