# The Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act 2013 (Commencement No. 10) Order 2016

Made: 19th April 2016

The Treasury make the following Order in exercise of the powers conferred by section 148(5) and (6) of the Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act 2013[^f00001]:

#### Citation and interpretation

###### 1

- (1) This Order may be cited as the Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act 2013 (Commencement No. 10) Order 2016.

- (2) In this Order—

- “the Act” means the Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act 2013;

- “FSMA” means the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000[^f00002].

#### Appointed date for the coming into force of certain provisions of the Act

###### 2

21st April 2016 is the day appointed for the coming into force of—

- (a) section 4 (ring-fencing of certain activities) of the Act for the purpose of inserting section 142H (ring-fencing rules) of FSMA; and

- (b) section 133 (power of the FCA and PRA to make rules applying to parent undertakings) of the Act for the purpose of inserting section 192JA (rules applying to parent undertakings of ring fenced bodies) of FSMA.

## Signed

Charlie Elphicke — Guto Bebb — Two of the Lords Commissioners of Her Majesty’s Treasury — 19th April 2016

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Order)

EXPLANATORY NOTE

This Order brings into force on 21st April 2016 certain provisions of the Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act [2013 (c. 33)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/33) (“the Act”).

Section 4 (ring-fencing of certain activities) of the Act (partially commenced on 1st March 2014) is brought into force for the additional purpose of inserting section 142H (ring-fencing rules) of the Financial Services and Markets Act [2000 (c. 8)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8) (“FSMA”). Section 133 (power of the FCA and PRA to make rules applying to parent undertakings) of the Act (partially commenced on 31st December 2014) is brought into force for the additional purpose of inserting section 192JA (rules applying to parent undertakings of ring fenced bodies). Sections 142H and 192JA of FSMA relate to the appropriate regulator’s powers to make rules for ring-fenced bodies and parent undertakings of ring-fenced bodies. A ring fenced body is a UK institution that falls within the definition in section 142A of FSMA. This is the tenth commencement order to be made under the Act.

An impact assessment of the effect that ring fencing will have on the costs of business, the voluntary sector and the public sector has been prepared and is available on HM Treasury’s website (www.gov.uk/treasury) or from HM Treasury, 1 Horse Guards Road, London, SW1A 2HQ. No separate impact assessment has been produced for this Order.
