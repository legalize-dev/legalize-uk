# The Local Authorities (Categorisation) (England) Order 2004

Made: 5th July 2004

Laid before Parliament: 8th July 2004

Coming into force: 29th July 2004

The First Secretary of State, having received a report from the Audit Commission[^f00001] produced under section 99(1) of the Local Government Act 2003[^f00002] in exercise of the powers conferred upon him by section 99(4) of that Act, hereby makes the following Order:

#### Citation, commencement and application

###### 1

- (1) This Order may be cited as the Local Authorities (Categorisation) (England) Order 2004 and shall come into force on 29th July 2004.

- (2) This Order applies in relation to English local authorities[^f00003].

#### Categorisation report

###### 2

The English local authorities, to which the report of the Audit Commission dated 1st July 2004 relates, are by this Order categorised in accordance with their categorisation in that report, as provided below.

#### Excellent authorities

###### 3

The local authorities listed in Schedule 1 to this Order are categorised as excellent.

#### Good authorities

###### 4

The local authorities listed in Schedule 2 to this Order are categorised as good.

#### Fair authorities

###### 5

The local authorities listed in Schedule 3 to this Order are categorised as fair.

#### Weak authorities

###### 6

The local authorities listed in Schedule 4 to this Order are categorised as weak.

#### Poor authorities

###### 7

The local authorities listed in Schedule 5 to this Order are categorised as poor.

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order categorises local authorities in England into five categories according to performance. The categorisation follows a report of the Audit Commission on 1st July 2004. A copy of the report may be obtained free of charge from Andrew Whitehead, Office of the Deputy Prime Minister, Eland House, Bressenden Place London SW1E 5DU; telephone 020 7944 3846 or email Andrew.Whitehead@odpm.gsi.gov.uk.

A regulatory impact assessment has not been prepared for this instrument as it has no impact on business, charities or voluntary bodies.
