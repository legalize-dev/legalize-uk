# The Rules of the Supreme Court (Amendment No. 2) 1981

Made: 30th November 1981

Laid before Parliament: 10th December 1981

Coming into Operation-

We, the Supreme Court Rule Committee, having power under section 84 of the Supreme Court Act 1981 to make rules of court for the purpose of regulating and prescribing the practice and procedure to be followed in the Supreme Court, hereby exercise those powers as follows:—

### Citation and commencement

###### 1

- (1) These Rules may be cited as the Rules of the Supreme Court (Amendment No. 2) 1981 and shall come into operation on 1st January 1982, except for Rules 2 to 24, which shall come into operation on 20th April 1982.

- (2) In these Rules an Order referred to by number means the Order so numbered in the Rules of the Supreme Court 1965[^f00001] and, unless the context otherwise requires, a form referred to by number means the form so numbered in Appendix A or, as the case may be, Appendix B to those Rules.

## Explanatory note

EXPLANATORY NOTE

These Rules amend the Rules of the Supreme Court so as—

1. to give effect to section 58 of the Supreme Court Act 1981 which enables the incidental jurisdiction of the Court of Appeal to be exercised either by a single judge or by the new registrar of civil appeals (Rules 2 to 24);

2. to give effect to sections 75A to 75C of the County Courts Act 1959 as amended by Schedule 3 to the Supreme Court Act 1981, which relate to the transfer of proceedings between the High Court and county courts (Rules 25 to 27);

3. to revise the fixed costs which may be allowed under Appendix 3 to Order 62 (Rules 28 to 30);

4. to give effect to section 40 of the Supreme Court Act 1981 which extends the scope of garnishee proceedings, and to provide better protection to garnishees (Rules 31 to 35);

5. to give effect to section 21 of the Supreme Court Act 1981 which provides for the mode of exercise of the Admiralty jurisdiction of the High Court, and to effect other minor amendments in relation to Admiralty proceedings (Rules 36 to 39).
