# The Public Record Office (Fees) Regulations 1992

Made: 4th December 1992

Coming into force: 1st January 1993

The Lord Chancellor, in exercise of the powers conferred on him by section 2(5) of the Public Records Act 1958[^f00001] and with the concurrence of the Treasury, hereby makes the following Regulations:

###### 1

These Regulations may be cited as the Public Record Office (Fees) Regulations 1992 and shall come into force on 1st January 1993.

###### 2

- (1) Subject to the following provision, the fees to be charged for the authentica tion of copies of or extracts from records under the charge of the Keeper of Public Records and for other services afforded by officers of the Public Record Office shall be those set out in column A of the Schedule to these Regulations in place of those set out, where these are at present applicable, in column B.

- (2) The Keeper of Public Records may remit a fee where the service performed by him is likely to include any research in consequence of a postal inquiry or has been or is likely to be exceptionally simple.

###### 3

The Public Record Office (Fees) Regulations 1990[^f00002] are hereby revoked.

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

The Regulations revoke the Public Record Office (Fees) Regulations 1990 and prescribe a fresh range of fees to be charged for authentication of copies of records and for other services provided by the Public Record Office. The new fees result from a full recosting of each service provided by the Public Record Office rather than the application of an across the board increase to existing fee levels. In addition the fees have been restructured so as more accurately to reflect both the nature of the services the Public Record Office now provides and the way in which they are now offered. In consequence some of the new fees have no direct equivalent which can be shown in the old fee column in the Schedule to the Regulations. In addition, photographs of records and the hand colouring of maps are services no longer offered; and so the charges previously levied for them have been omitted from these Regulations.
