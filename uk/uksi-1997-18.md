# The Rules of Procedure (Army) (Amendment No. 2) Rules 1997

Made: 8th January 1997

Laid before Parliament: 9th January 1997

Coming into force: 1st February 1997

The Secretary of State, in exercise of the powers conferred on him by sections 103 and 209 of the Army Act 1955[^f00001], hereby makes the following Rules:—

###### 1

These Rules may be cited as the Rules of Procedure (Army) (Amendment No. 2) Rules 1997 and shall come into force on 1st February 1997.

###### 2

In the Rules of Procedure (Army) 1972[^f00002], in rule 9(d), for the words which the accused is to be asked there shall be substituted the following words:

> > - Do you wish to say anything? You do not have to say anything but it may harm your defence if you do not mention now something which you later rely on in court. If you do wish to say anything, it must be evidence given on oath. Any evidence you give will be taken down in writing and may be given in evidence in court.

## Signed

James Arbuthnot — Minister of State, Ministry of Defence — 8th January 1997

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Rules)

These Rules amend the Rules of Procedure (Army) 1972 in consequence of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (Application to the Armed Forces) Order 1997 (type=start slip=1997-02-01 time=1188313371557[S.I. 1997/16](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/16)type=end slip=1997-02-01 time=1188313371557) which applies sections 34 to 38 of the 1994 Act to the Armed Forces. The amendment substitutes a new form of words which an accused is to be asked under Rule 9(d) when evidence is being taken. The new words will inform the accused that it may harm his defence not to mention something which he later relies on in court.
