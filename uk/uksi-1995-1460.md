# The Inheritance Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1995

Made: 7th June 1995

Laid before the House of Commons: 8th June 1995

Coming into force: 1st July 1995

The Commissioners of Inland Revenue, in exercise of the powers conferred on them by section 256(1)(a) of the Inheritance Tax Act 1984[^f00001], hereby make the following Regulations:

###### 1

- (1) These Regulations may be cited as the Inheritance Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1995 and shall come into force on 1st July 1995.

- (2) These Regulations extend to Northern Ireland only.

###### 2

In regulation 3 of the Capital Transfer Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1981[^f00002]—

- (a) in paragraph (b) of the definition of “an excepted estate” for “£125,000” there shall be substituted “£145,000”;

- (b) in paragraph (d) of that defintion for “1st April 1991” there shall be substituted “6th April 1995”.

## Signed

S C T Matheson — G H Bush — Two of the Commissioners of Inland Revenue — 7th June 1995

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

Regulation 4 of the Capital Transfer Tax (Delivery of Accounts) (Northern Ireland) Regulations 1981 (S.I. [1981/1441](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1981/1441)) (“the principal Regulations”) provides that a person is not required to deliver an account of the property comprised in an excepted estate for inheritance tax purposes. (By virtue of section 100 of the Finance Act [1986 (c. 41)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1986/41) capital transfer tax is now known as inheritance tax.)

An excepted estate is defined in regulation 3 of the principal Regulations as an estate where (subject to various other conditions) the total gross value of the estate of a deceased immediately before his death does not exceed a specified amount. Subsequent amending Regulations have raised that specified amount from time to time.

These Regulations make a further amendment to that specified amount, raising it from £125,000 to £145,000 in respect of deaths on or after 6th April 1995.
