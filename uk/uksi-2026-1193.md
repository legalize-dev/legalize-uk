# The Statement of Trade Union Rights Regulations 2026

Made: 12th November 2026

Laid before Parliament: 16th November 2026

Coming into force: 1st January 2027

The Secretary of State makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 136A(2)(b), (3), (4) and (6) and 293(1) and (2) of the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992[^f00001].

#### Citation, commencement and extent

###### 1

- (1) These Regulations may be cited as the Statement of Trade Union Rights Regulations 2026.

- (2) These Regulations come into force on 1st January 2027.

- (3) These Regulations extend to England and Wales and Scotland.

#### Interpretation

###### 2

In these Regulations—

- “*the 1992 Act*” means the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992;

- “*Certification Officer*” means the officer referred to in section 254(1) and appointed in accordance with section 254(2) of the 1992 Act[^f00002];

- “*employment*” has the same meaning as in section 230(5) of the Employment Rights Act 1996[^f00003] and “*employed*” is to be construed accordingly;

- “*statement*” means the written statement required to be given under section 136A of the 1992 Act;

- “*statutory access agreement*” means an access agreement within the meaning of section 70ZA(2) of the 1992 Act[^f00004];

- “*year*” means a year beginning on 6th April in any year and ending on the following 5th April.

#### Form and information to be provided

###### 3

- (1) Whenever an employer gives a statement in accordance with regulation 4, 5, or 6, the statement must—

- (a) be in the form set out in the Schedule or a form substantially to the same effect, and

- (b) include all the information in or required by that Schedule, taking into account the italicised instructions and options in the Schedule.

- (2) No further information may be included in the statement and no modifications may be made to the information to be included in the statement as set out in the Schedule except in accordance with paragraphs (3) to (7).

- (3) Where an employer[^f00005] recognises one or more trade unions[^f00006] for the purposes of collective bargaining[^f00007], and whether or not the collective bargaining applies to the worker[^f00008] to whom the statement is to be given, the employer must modify the statement as set out in the Schedule to include the information referred to in the Schedule relating to each trade union so recognised.

- (4) Where an employer does not recognise any trade union for the purposes of collective bargaining, the employer must modify the statement as set out in the Schedule to include the wording specified in the Schedule for such a case.

- (5) Where an employer has a statutory access agreement with one or more trade unions, and whether or not the statutory access agreement applies to the worker to whom the statement is to be given, the employer must modify the statement as set out in the Schedule to include the information referred to in the Schedule relating to each such trade union.

- (6) Where an employer does not have any statutory access agreement, the employer must modify the statement as set out in the Schedule to include the wording specified in the Schedule for such a case.

- (7) An employer must do at least one of the following—

- (a) modify the statement as set out in the Schedule—

- (i) where the statement is sent or made available to the worker by electronic means, to include a hyperlink to, and the address of, the following webpage on which the Certification Officer’s list of trade unions kept under section 2 of the 1992 Act is published: https://www.gov.uk/certification-officer-list-of-trade-unions, or

- (ii) where the statement is sent or made available to the worker other than by electronic means, to include the address of that webpage;

- (b) provide the Certification Officer’s list of trade unions kept under section 2 of the 1992 Act to the worker alongside the statement and modify the statement as set out in the Schedule to refer to the provision of that list.

- (8) Where an employer provides the Certification Officer’s list alongside the statement in accordance with paragraph (7)(b), the employer must obtain the list from the webpage stated in paragraph (7)(a)(i) or otherwise from the Certification Officer not more than one month before the employer provides the list to the worker.

#### First provision: timing and manner of delivery

###### 4

- (1) Where a worker begins employment with an employer at any time on or after 1st January 2027, the employer must give the statement to the worker at the time required by section 136A(2)(a) of the 1992 Act by—

- (a) delivering it to the worker in person,

- (b) sending it to the worker by post, or

- (c) sending it to the worker by electronic means.

- (2) Where a worker was in employment with an employer immediately before 1st January 2027 and remains employed by that employer on 5th April 2027, the employer must on or before 5th April 2027—

- (a) give the statement to the worker by—

- (i) delivering it to the worker in person,

- (ii) sending it to the worker by post, or

- (iii) sending it to the worker by electronic means, or

- (b) give the statement by making it available to the worker in a manner that ensures it is reasonably accessible to the worker.

- (3) Where an employer gives the statement to a worker in accordance with paragraph (2)(b), the employer must—

- (a) on or before 5th April 2027, notify the worker in writing that the statement is available and of the location at which the worker may access it, including sufficient information to enable the worker to find that location,

- (b) ensure that the statement remains continuously available and reasonably accessible to the worker from the time the notification under sub-paragraph (a) is given until the end of 5th April 2027, and

- (c) ensure that the statement can be accessed without the worker making a specific request.

- (4) The employer may, on one or more occasions, change the location notified to the worker provided that—

- (a) if the previous location ceases to be reasonably accessible to the worker for a reason beyond the employer’s reasonable control, the employer notifies the worker in writing of the new location, including sufficient information to enable the worker to find that location, as soon as reasonably possible, or

- (b) in any other case, the employer notifies the worker in writing, before but not more than one month before the change takes effect, of—

- (i) the new location, including sufficient information to enable the worker to find that location, and

- (ii) the date on which the change takes effect.

- (5) A notification under paragraphs (3)(a) and (4)—

- (a) must be headed, “Availability and location of statement of trade union rights”, and

- (b) must not qualify or contradict the information contained in the statement.

- (6) A notification under paragraphs (3)(a) and (4) must be—

- (a) delivered to the worker in person,

- (b) sent to the worker by post, or

- (c) sent to the worker by electronic means.

#### Subsequent provision: timing and manner of delivery

###### 5

- (1) This regulation applies in relation to a worker to whom an employer is required to give a statement in accordance with regulation 4.

- (2) While the worker remains employed by that employer, the employer must in each year subsequent to the year in which the statement was required to be given in accordance with regulation 4—

- (a) on or before 5th April, give the statement again to the worker by—

- (i) delivering it to the worker in person,

- (ii) sending it to the worker by post, or

- (iii) sending it to the worker by electronic means, or

- (b) give the statement again by making it available to the worker in a manner that ensures it is reasonably accessible to the worker.

- (3) Where an employer gives the statement to a worker in accordance with paragraph (2)(b), the employer must—

- (a) at any time before the start of the year and unless the worker was notified of these matters in the previous year under paragraph (3)(a) of regulation 4 or paragraph (3)(b) of this regulation, notify the worker in writing that the statement is available and of the location at which the worker may access it, including sufficient information to enable the worker to find that location,

- (b) on or before 5th April, notify the worker in writing again that the statement is available and of the location at which the worker may access it, including sufficient information to enable the worker to find that location,

- (c) ensure that the statement remains continuously available and reasonably accessible to the worker for the whole of the year, and

- (d) ensure that the statement can be accessed without the worker making a specific request.

- (4) The employer may, on one or more occasions, change the location notified to the worker provided that—

- (a) if the previous location ceases to be reasonably accessible to the worker for a reason beyond the employer’s reasonable control, the employer notifies the worker in writing of the new location, including sufficient information to enable the worker to find that location, as soon as reasonably possible, or

- (b) in any other case, the employer notifies the worker in writing, before but not more than one month before the change takes effect, of—

- (i) the new location, including sufficient information to enable the worker to find that location, and

- (ii) the date on which the change takes effect.

- (5) A notification under paragraphs (3)(a) and (b) and (4)—

- (a) must be headed, “Availability and location of statement of trade union rights”, and

- (b) must not qualify or contradict the information contained in the statement.

- (6) A notification under paragraphs (3)(a) and (b) and (4) of this regulation must be—

- (a) delivered to the worker in person,

- (b) sent to the worker by post, or

- (c) sent to the worker by electronic means.

#### Updated statements: timing and manner of delivery

###### 6

- (1) Where there is a change to the information required to be included in the statement by paragraphs (3) to (6) of regulation 3, an employer must, before the end of the period of one month beginning with the date on which the change takes effect, give an updated statement to a worker if—

- (a) the employer has previously given the statement to that worker, and

- (b) the worker remains employed by the employer.

- (2) Paragraph (1) does not require an employer to give an updated statement solely because of a change to the contact details of a trade union if, in the same year, the employer has already given updated statements in respect of two changes to the contact details of that trade union.

- (3) An employer must comply with paragraph (1)—

- (a) where, at the time the change takes effect, the statement is being made continuously available and reasonably accessible to the worker in accordance with regulation 4(3) or regulation 5(3), by amending the statement so made available, or

- (b) otherwise by—

- (i) delivering the updated statement to the worker in person,

- (ii) sending the updated statement to the worker by post, or

- (iii) sending the updated statement to the worker by electronic means.

- (4) Where an employer complies with paragraph (1) in accordance with paragraph (3)(a), the employer is not required to notify the worker about the change or the updated statement.

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

Explanatory Note

These Regulations make provision about the written statement which an employer is required to give to a worker under section 136A of the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992 (“*the 1992 Act*”). Section 136A was inserted into the 1992 Act by section 58 of the Employment Rights Act 2025 (“*the 2025 Act*”).

Where a worker brings successful proceedings to an employment tribunal under the jurisdictions set out in Schedule 5 to the Employment Act 2002 (the “*2002 Act*”) and, when the proceedings were begun, the employer was in breach of duty to the worker under section 136A of the 1992 Act, the tribunal may make an award or increased award to the worker of two weeks’ pay or four weeks’ pay (see section 38 of the 2002 Act, as amended by section 58 of the 2025 Act).

Regulation 3 and the Schedule prescribe the form of the statement and the information to be included in it. Regulation 3 also makes provision for information to be included in the statement relating to recognised trade unions and trade unions with which an employer has a statutory access agreement, and about the Certification Officer’s list of trade unions.

The address of the webpage referred to in regulation 3(7)(a), on which the Certification Officer’s list of trade unions kept under section 2 of the 1992 Act is found is as follows: https://www.gov.uk/certification-officer-list-of-trade-unions. The list can also be obtained by writing to the Certification Officer at Certification Office, 11th Floor, 14 Westfield Avenue, London, E20 1HZ or at info@certoffice.org. The Certification Officer will aim to respond to any requests for the list as soon as reasonably possible and in any event within 14 days of receiving a request.

Regulation 4 makes provision about the timing and manner in which an employer must give the first statement to workers who begin employment at any time on or after 1st January 2027 and to workers already in employment immediately before that date and who remain employed on 5th April 2027.

Regulation 5 makes provision about the timing and manner in which an employer must subsequently give a statement to a worker. It also provides for the statement to remain continuously available and reasonably accessible to a worker instead of being given again in person, by post or by electronic means, and for changes to the location at which the statement may be accessed.

Regulation 6 makes provision for an employer to give a worker an updated statement where there is a change to the information required to be included in the statement about recognised trade unions or statutory access agreements. It also limits the frequency with which an updated statement is required solely because of changes to a trade union’s contact details.

A full impact assessment of the effect that this instrument will have on the private, voluntary and the public sectors has been prepared and is available from the Employment Rights Directorate, Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade, Old Admiralty Building, London SW1A 2AY and is annexed to the Explanatory Memorandum which is available alongside the instrument on www.legislation.gov.uk.
