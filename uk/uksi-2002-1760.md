# The National Institute for Clinical Excellence (Establishment and Constitution) Amendment Order 2002

Made: 9th July 2002

Laid before Parliament: 9th July 2002

Coming into force: 1st August 2002

The Secretary of State for Health and the National Assembly for Wales, acting concurrently in exercise of powers conferred by sections 11(1), (2) and (4) and 126(3) and (4) of the National Health Service Act 1977[^f00001] and now vested in them and of all other powers enabling them in that behalf, hereby make the following Order:

#### Citation, application and commencement

###### 1

This Order which may be cited as the National Institute for Clinical Excellence (Establishment and Constitution) Amendment Order 2002 applies to England and Wales and shall come into force on 1st August 2002.

#### Amendment of the National Institute for Clinical Excellence (Establishment and Constitution) Order 1999

###### 2

- (1) Article 4 of the National Institute for Clinical Excellence (Establishment and Constitution) Order 1999[^f00002] (constitution of the Institute) is amended as follows—

- (a) in paragraph (b) for the word “seven” substitute the word “eight”;

- (b) in paragraph (c) for the word “four” substitute the word “five”;

- (c) after paragraph (c) add a new paragraph—

> > (2) The members mentioned in paragraph (1)(b) above shall include a person with particular experience suited to the interests of Wales.

## Signed

Signed on behalf of the National Assembly for Wales undersection 66(1) of the Government of Wales Act 1998[^f00003]

Dafydd Ellis Thomas — The Presiding Officer of the National Assembly — 4th July 2002

Hunt — Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State, — Department of Health — 9th July 2002

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Order)

This Order amends the National Institute for Clinical Excellence (Establishment and Constitution) Order 1999 to increase the number of members of the Institute who are not officers of the Institute from seven to eight and to specify that one such member shall have experience suited to the interests of Wales and to increase the number of members of the Institute who are officers of the Institute from four to five.
