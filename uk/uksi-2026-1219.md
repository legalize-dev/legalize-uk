# The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026

Made: 18th November 2026

Coming into force in accordance with regulation 1(2) and (3)

The Treasury make these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 21(5), (6), (9), (10) and (15), 22(1) and (5), 71K, 71M, and 428(3) of, and paragraph 25 of Schedule 2 to, the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (“*the Act*”)[^f00001].

In the opinion of the Treasury, one of the effects of these Regulations is that an activity which is not a regulated activity (within the meaning given by section 22 of the Act) will become a regulated activity.

In accordance with section 429(3) of, and paragraph 26(2)(a) of Schedule 2 to, the Act, a draft of these Regulations has been laid before, and approved by a resolution of, each House of Parliament.

## Part 1 — General

#### Citation, commencement and extent

###### 1

- (1) These Regulations may be cited as the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026.

- (2) Subject to paragraph (3), these Regulations come into force the day after the day on which they are made.

- (3) Regulations 2 and 3 come into force on 25th October 2027.

- (4) These Regulations extend to England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

## Part 2 — Amendment of secondary legislation

#### Amendment of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Regulated Activities) Order 2001

###### 2

- (1) The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Regulated Activities) Order 2001[^f00002] is amended as follows.

- (2) After article 43 (activities not constituting administration) insert—

> > (43A) There are excluded from article 40 (safeguarding and administering investments) arrangements—
>   (a) made in relation to a UK qualifying stablecoin,
>   (b) carried on by a person with a Part 4A permission to carry on the regulated activity specified by article 9M (issuing qualifying stablecoin) in relation to that UK qualifying stablecoin, and
>   (c) relating to assets held for the purpose set out in article 88G(2)(b) (qualifying stablecoin).

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

Explanatory Note

These Regulations amend and add to the regulatory regime for certain cryptoasset activities pursuant to powers conferred by the Financial Services and Markets Act [2000 (c. 8)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8) (“*the Act*”).

Part 1 of the Regulations sets out the citation, commencement, extent of the Regulations.

Part 2 of the Regulations amends other Regulations. Regulation 1 amends the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Regulated Activities) Order 2001 ([S.I. 2001/544](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/544)), regulation 2 amends the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Financial Promotion) Order 2005 ([S.I. 2005/1529](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/1529)) and regulation 3 amends the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 ([S.I. 2026/102](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/102)).

These amendments amend both regulated activities and controlled activities, the latter in relation to financial promotions, relating to cryptoassets for the purposes of regulation under the Act.

The Regulations also provide for the early commencement of amendments to the following instruments made in the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026—

- the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Collective Investment Schemes) Order 2001;

- the Electronic Money Regulations 2011;

- the Alternative Investment Fund Managers Regulations 2013.

No impact assessment for these Regulations has been prepared, as no significant impacts on the costs of business, the voluntary sector and the public sector are envisaged beyond those already considered in the impact assessment for the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026, which is available from HM Treasury, 1 Horse Guards Road, London, SW1A 2HQ and was published alongside these Regulations at www.legislation.gov.uk.
