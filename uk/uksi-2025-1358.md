# The Infected Blood Compensation Scheme (Amendment) Regulations 2025

Made: 16th December 2025

Coming into force: 31st December 2025

The Minister for the Cabinet Office makes these Regulations in exercise of the powers conferred by sections 49(1), (2) and (4), 50 and 79(1) of the Victims and Prisoners Act 2024[^f00001].

In accordance with section 79(10) of the Victims and Prisoners Act 2024, a draft of this instrument was laid before Parliament and approved by a resolution of each House of Parliament.

#### Citation, commencement and extent

###### 1

- (1) These Regulations may be cited as the Infected Blood Compensation Scheme (Amendment) Regulations 2025.

- (2) These Regulations come into force on 31st December 2025.

- (3) These Regulations extend to England and Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland.

#### Amendment of the Infected Blood Compensation Scheme Regulations 2025

###### 2

The Infected Blood Compensation Scheme Regulations 2025[^f00002] are amended in accordance with regulations 3 to 14.

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

Explanatory Note

The infected blood compensation scheme (“the IBCS”) was established by the Infected Blood Compensation Scheme Regulations 2024 ([S.I. 2024/872](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/872)). The IBCS was subsequently expanded by the Infected Blood Compensation Scheme Regulations 2025 ([S.I. 2025/404](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/404)) which revoked and replaced [S.I. 2024/872](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2024/872).

These Regulations amend [S.I. 2025/404](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/404) as follows—

1. regulation 3 modifies the eligibility criteria for infected persons so that— a person is eligible for compensation if they were infected with HIV as a result of infected blood treatment received before 1st January 1982 (see regulation 3(10) of [S.I. 2025/404](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/404) for the meaning of “infected blood treatment”); there are consequential amendments to the methods for calculating the amounts of associated awards for both infected and affected persons; a person is automatically eligible for compensation if they are receiving support scheme payments as an infected person (see regulation 2(1) for the meaning of “support scheme payments”); a person (“*A*”) is eligible for compensation if a Hepatitis B infection was transmitted to them by a person (“*B*”) who does not satisfy either of the conditions in regulation 3(3)(c)(i) and (ii) of [S.I. 2025/404](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2025/404), as long as A does satisfy those conditions and as long as B satisfies all other eligibility criteria; a person is not eligible for compensation if they became infected by virtue of a tissue transplant where they were notified before the transplant that the tissue carried an infection; a person is eligible for compensation if they were in a long-term relationship with another person (“*B*”), became infected via sexual contact with B, were not living with B at the time of the contact, and then subsequently lived with B;

2. regulation 4 modifies the eligibility criteria for affected persons so that— the estate of an affected person can claim compensation if that person died or dies during the period which begins with 21st May 2024 and ends with 31st March 2031; a person is automatically eligible for compensation if they are receiving support scheme payments as an affected person;

3. regulation 5 modifies the definition of “relevant person” to ensure that payments are made directly to, and requirements to repay are imposed directly on, an eligible person who lacks capacity (and not to someone holding a power of attorney or similar authority, as previously);

4. regulation 6 removes the requirement for applications to be accompanied by evidence about the date of Hepatitis infections;

5. regulation 7 ensures that a person who suffers the most severe level of Hepatitis infection, and whose level of infection severity for past years must be deemed because of an absence of evidence, is deemed to be at level 3 severity for the intended amount of time (whereas previously they might have been deemed never to have been at level 3);

6. regulation 8 establishes that a person cannot receive compensation for end-of-life care costs twice;

7. regulation 9 removes the minimum earnings threshold for a person to claim exceptional loss as a result of reduced earnings caused by an infection;

8. regulation 10 delays the time at which support scheme payments will be paid under the IBCS instead of under infected blood support schemes;

9. regulation 11 secures that bereaved partners of recently deceased infected persons receive, for a period of 12 months after the infected person’s death, 100% of the value of the support scheme payments that the infected person was entitled to (instead of the 75% which the bereaved partner would otherwise receive);

10. regulation 12 allows— a person who is receiving their compensation as periodic payments to opt to end the periodic payments and receive the outstanding amount as a lump sum, and a person who opted to continue to receive support scheme payments to change their mind, so that the amount of compensation they would have been entitled to had they opted out of support scheme payments becomes payable to them;

11. regulation 13 modifies the description of psychiatric conditions so that now an eligible infected person with any secondary psychotic disorder caused by their infection or interferon treatment might qualify for compensation;

12. regulation 14 makes corrections and miscellaneous minor amendments.

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as no, or no significant, impact on the private, voluntary or public sector is foreseen.
