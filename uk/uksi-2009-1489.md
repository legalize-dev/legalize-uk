# The Social Fund Winter Fuel Payment (Temporary Increase) Regulations 2009

Made: 12th June 2009

Laid before Parliament: 18th June 2009

Coming into force: 21st September 2009

The Social Security Advisory Committee has agreed that proposals in respect of these Regulations should not be referred to it[^f00002].

#### Citation and commencement

###### 1

- (1) These Regulations may be cited as the Social Fund Winter Fuel Payment (Temporary Increase) Regulations 2009.

- (2) They come into force on 21st September 2009.

#### Modification of the Social Fund Winter Fuel Payment Regulations 2000

###### 2

- (1) For the purpose of making social fund winter fuel payments in respect of the 2009–10 winter, regulation 2 of the Social Fund Winter Fuel Payment Regulations 2000[^f00003] (social fund winter fuel payments) is applied as if—

- (a) the references to £100 (in both places) were to £125;

- (b) in paragraphs (1)(i), (2)(a) and (3), the reference to £200 were to £250;

- (c) in paragraph (2)(b), the reference to £200 were to £275;

- (d) in paragraph (2)(b), the reference to £150 were to £200; and

- (e) the references to £300 (in both places) were to £400.

- (2) In this regulation, “the 2009–10 winter” means the winter which follows the qualifying week beginning on 21st September 2009.

#### Revocation

###### 3

The Social Fund Winter Fuel Payment (Temporary Increase) Regulations 2008[^f00004] are revoked.

## Signed

Signed by authority of the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions.

Angela Eagle — Minister of State, — Department for Work and Pensions — 12th June 2009

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

EXPLANATORY NOTE

These Regulations modify the Social Fund Winter Fuel Payment Regulations 2000 to provide for an increase in winter fuel payments for winter 2009/2010.

They also revoke the Social Fund Winter Fuel Payment (Temporary Increase) Regulations 2008 which provided for an increase in payments for winter 2008/2009.

A full impact assessment has not been produced for this instrument as it has no impact on the private or voluntary sectors.
