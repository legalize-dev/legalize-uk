# The Public Bodies (Abolition of Courts Boards) Order 2012

Made: 1st May 2012

Coming into force in accordance with article 1

A draft of this Order, and an explanatory document containing the information required by section 11(2) of that Act, have been laid before Parliament in accordance with section 11(1) after the end of the period of twelve weeks mentioned in section 11(3). In accordance with section 11(4) of that Act, the draft of this Order has been approved by a resolution of each House of Parliament after the expiry of the 40-day period referred to in that provision.

#### Citation, commencement and extent

###### 1

- (1) This Order may be cited as the Public Bodies (Abolition of Courts Boards) Order 2012.

- (2) Subject to paragraph [(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/1206/article/1/3/enacted), this Order comes into force on the later of 1st April 2012 or the day after the day on which it is made.

- (3) Article [3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2012/1206/article/3/enacted) comes into force on the day after that on which the other provisions of this Order come into force.

- (4) This Order extends to England and Wales.

#### Abolition of courts boards

###### 2

- (1) Courts boards, established under section 4 of the Courts Act 2003[^f00002], are abolished.

- (2) The Schedule (which makes consequential provision) has effect.

#### Repeal of entry in the Public Bodies Act 2011

###### 3

In Schedule 1 to the Public Bodies Act 2011 (power to abolish: bodies and offices), the entry “Courts boards.” is repealed.

## SCHEDULE — Amendments consequential on the abolition of courts boards

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Order)

EXPLANATORY NOTE

This Order abolishes courts boards, which were established by section 4 of the Courts Act 2003.

The Schedule to this Order makes legislative amendments which are consequential on the abolition.

A regulatory impact assessment has not been prepared for this Order as there is no impact on business, civil society or regulatory matters, and costs/benefits to the public sector will not exceed £5 million per annum.
