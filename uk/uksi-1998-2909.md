# The Legal Aid in Criminal and Care Proceedings (General) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 1998

Made: 19th November 1998

Laid before Parliament: 25th November 1998

Coming into force: 4th January 1999

The Lord Chancellor, in exercise of the powers conferred on him by sections 21(5), 23, 34 and 43 of the Legal Aid Act 1988[^f00001], with the consent of the Treasury, makes the following Regulations:

#### Citation and commencement

###### 1

These Regulations may be cited as the Legal Aid in Criminal and Care Proceedings (General) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations 1998 and shall come into force on 4th January 1999.

#### Interpretation

###### 2

In these Regulations “the Regulations” means the Legal Aid in Criminal and Care Proceedings (General) Regulations 1989[^f00002] and a reference to a regulation or Schedule by number alone means the regulation or Schedule so numbered in the Regulations.

#### Amendments to the Legal Aid in Criminal and Care Proceedings (General) Regulations 1989

- (2) After regulation 11(3) the following paragraph shall be inserted:

###### 14

> > (1A) A justices' clerk to whom an application for legal aid has been made under regulation 18(1)(f) may do one of the following:

>     (i) the proceedings in the magistrates' court in which the court decides whether or not to send the defendant to the Crown Court for trial;
>     (ii) the defendant’s trial before the Crown Court, in the event of his being sent to the Crown Court for trial; and
>     (iii) the defendant’s trial in a magistrates' court in the event of his being remitted for trial under paragraph 10(3)(a) or 13(2) of Schedule 3 to the Crime and Disorder Act 1998;

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

These Regulations amend the Legal Aid in Criminal and Care Proceedings (General) Regulations 1989 so that where a defendant is liable to be sent for trial under section 51 of the Crime and Disorder Act 1998, he may apply for legal aid to the sending magistrates' court, which can grant legal aid for all subsequent proceedings, including the trial. When such an application is made, the defendant must, as previously, file a statement of means, but is not obliged to provide supporting documents at the same time. When the supporting documents have been supplied a redetermination of the defendant’s means is carried out.
