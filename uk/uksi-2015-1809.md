# The Serious Crime Act 2015 (Commencement No. 3) Regulations 2015

Made: 20th October 2015

The Secretary of State, in exercise of the power conferred by section 88(1) of the Serious Crime Act 2015[^f00001], makes the following Regulations.

#### Citation and interpretation

###### 1

- (1) These Regulations may be cited as the Serious Crime Act 2015 (Commencement No. 3) Regulations 2015.

- (2) In these Regulations “the 2015 Act” means the Serious Crime Act 2015.

#### Provisions coming into force on 31st October 2015

###### 2

The following provisions of the 2015 Act come into force on 31st October 2015—

- (a) section 74 (duty to notify police of female genital mutilation);

- (b) section 75 (guidance about female genital mutilation);

- (c) section 85(1) (minor and consequential amendments), for the purposes of paragraph 60 of Schedule 4 (minor and consequential amendments); and

- (d) paragraph 60 of Schedule 4.

#### Provision coming into force on 10th November 2015

###### 3

Section 79 of the 2015 Act (throwing articles into prisons) comes into force on 10th November 2015.

## Signed

Karen Bradley — Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State — Home Office — 20th October 2015

## Explanatory note

(This note is not part of the Regulations)

EXPLANATORY NOTE

These Regulations are the third commencement regulations made under the Serious Crime Act [2015 (c. 9)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/9) (“the 2015 Act”).

Regulation 2 brings into force on 31st October 2015 sections 74 and 75 of the 2015 Act, which insert new sections 5B and 5C into the Female Genital Mutilation Act [2003 (c. 31)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/31) to create a duty to notify the police of female genital mutilation in England and Wales. Regulation 2 also brings into force on the same day the consequential amendment in paragraph 60 of Schedule 4 to the 2015 Act that relates to these provisions.

Regulation 3 brings into force on 10th November 2015 section 79 of the 2015 Act which inserts a new section 40CB into the Prison Act [1952 (c. 52)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1952/52), making it a criminal offence for a person, without authorisation, to throw any article or substance into a prison. The reference to “throwing” includes doing anything from outside the prison that results in the article or substance being projected or conveyed over or through a boundary of the prison so as to land inside the prison.
